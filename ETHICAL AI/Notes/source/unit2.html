1. Understanding Bias in Data and Models

Bias & fairnessBias = systematic, unfair skew in outcomes for certain people or groups. Fairness = absence of unjustified discrimination. The ethical principle at stake is Justice: AI should act justly and without bias.
  • Entry point (book §2.2): supervised learning needs human-labelled data, so human and societal biases can be baked in — a model trained on past decisions reproduces the past.
Where it comes from
Data biasUnrepresentative samples, skewed or prejudiced labels, missing groups, and proxy variables (neighbourhood ↔ race) that leak protected attributes +
Model biasPoor problem formulation / objective (cost used as proxy for need), algorithm choices, optimising overall accuracy while a minority performs badly +
Removing the sensitive column is not enoughOther features act as proxies ("fairness through unawareness" fails). Blinding algorithms to race can even hurt (Kleinberg et al.), yet data must still reflect "what is the case". Trade-offs between fairness and accuracy must be made consciously.

2. How Bias Impacts the Decision-Making Process

  • Consequences: unequal access to credit, jobs, healthcare and justice; loss of trust; violation of justice and non-maleficence; legal and reputational risk; one biased algorithm affects millions.
  • Automation bias: people over-trust an "objective" score +. Feedback loops: predictive policing → more patrols → more arrests → "more crime data" +.
Book's discussion questionWe expect AI to be fairer than humans — are there limits? Yes: it learns from human data, fairness has several conflicting definitions, and precise algorithms struggle with amorphous concepts.

5. Techniques to Detect Bias

  1. Audit the data — group-wise counts, label rates, missing values; correlate features with protected attributes to spot proxies.
  2. Compare outcomes across groups with fairness metrics (Section 6).
  3. Error analysis / slicing — accuracy, FPR, FNR per subgroup and intersections (race × gender) +.
  4. Explainability — feature importance, SHAP / LIME to expose proxy reliance +.
  5. Toolkits and audits (book §3.6.2): IBM AI Fairness 360, audit-AI, Facebook Fairness Flow, third-party audits (O'Neil Risk Consulting & Algorithmic Auditing), IEEE algorithmic-bias standards project (2017). Also Fairlearn and Google What-If Tool +.
  6. Diverse test cases / red-teaming and continuous monitoring after deployment.

6. Fairness Metrics +

G = protected group, Ŷ = prediction, Y = ground truth.

MetricRequiresIdea
Statistical (demographic) parityEqual selection rateP(Ŷ=1 | G=a) = P(Ŷ=1 | G=b); report the difference
Disparate impact ratioRatio ≥ 0.8 (80% rule)selection rate of unprivileged ÷ privileged
Equal opportunityEqual TPRQualified people approved at the same rate in every group
Equalised oddsEqual TPR and FPRBoth error rates equal across groups
Predictive parityEqual precisionP(Y=1 | Ŷ=1, G) same — COMPAS developers' choice
CalibrationSame risk per scoreP(Y=1 | score=s, G) same for all groups
Individual fairnessSimilar → similarSimilar individuals receive similar outcomes
Counterfactual fairnessInvarianceOutcome unchanged if the protected attribute is flipped (causal)
Metrics cannot all hold at onceUnless base rates are equal or the classifier is perfect, you cannot satisfy them all (the COMPAS dispute). Choose the metric that fits the context and the harm.

7. Techniques to Mitigate Bias +

StageTechniquePros / cons
Pre-processing
(fix the data)		More / representative data; re-sampling; reweighing (weight each group×label cell so group ⊥ label: w = P(g)·P(y) / P(g,y)); remove or transform proxies; disparate-impact removerModel-agnostic, cheap; may not remove proxy bias fully
In-processing
(fix the learning)		Add fairness constraints / regulariser to the loss; adversarial debiasing (adversary tries to predict group from output); fair representation learningDirectly trades fairness vs accuracy; needs custom training
Post-processing
(fix the output)		Group-specific thresholds; equalised-odds post-processing; reject-option classification (relabel near-boundary cases)Works on black-box models; can be legally sensitive
Non-technical measuresDiverse development teams • fairness requirements agreed with stakeholders • datasheets for datasets and model cards • human-in-the-loop review • appeal and redress mechanisms • regular audits • legal compliance (GDPR, anti-discrimination law) • honest handling of the fairness ↔ accuracy trade-off.

9. Quick Revision & Likely Questions

One-liners
  • Bias = systematic unfairness; enters via data (historical, sampling, labels, proxies) and model / objective. Removing the protected attribute ≠ fair.
  • Impact: unequal decisions in credit, hiring, health, justice at scale → loss of trust, legal and reputational risk.
  • Cases: COMPAS (conflicting definitions, 65% accuracy, Loomis) • healthcare cost proxy (Obermeyer) • credit / neighbourhood • Amazon hiring • Gender Shades.
  • Detect: data audit • parity / disparate impact (80%) / equal opportunity / equalised odds / predictive parity • subgroup errors • AIF360, Fairlearn, audits.
  • Mitigate: pre (re-sample, reweigh) • in (constraints, adversarial) • post (thresholds, reject option) + diverse teams, documentation, oversight.
  • Fairness metrics conflict → context decides; the fairness–accuracy trade-off is a stakeholder decision.
