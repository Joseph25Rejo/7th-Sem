1. Understanding Bias in Data and Models

Bias in data and modelsThe presence of systematic deviations that lead to inaccuracies or unfairness in decision-making. It emerges when data-collection or model-construction processes inadvertently favour certain groups, attributes or perspectives over others.
  • Sources: historical inequalities, flawed data-collection methods, biased algorithms.
  • Addressing it needs: a deep understanding of how bias shows up in both data and model outcomes, plus strategies that ensure equitable, unbiased decision making.
  • Detecting and mitigating bias is empowered by technology; the goal is transparent, responsible AI that champions diversity and societal progress.

2. Importance of Understanding Bias

WhyExplanation
Avoiding discrimination & inequityBias in data or models can produce discriminatory outcomes; understanding its origins lets developers treat all individuals impartially regardless of background, gender or race.
Ensuring ethical AI deploymentLets developers align work with ethical principles and legal mandates — steer clear of accentuating societal biases; strive for fairness, transparency, accountability.
Building trustSystems that keep producing biased outcomes erode public trust; addressing bias builds credibility and the belief that AI functions equitably.
Better decision makingAI now drives hiring, lending, criminal justice; understanding bias keeps those decisions informed, transparent and free of discriminatory influence.
Promoting innovationA system tainted by bias may fail to represent the spectrum of human experience; fixing bias makes AI adaptive, versatile and potent across contexts.
Reducing reproduction of historical injusticesAI learning from biased data can inherit negative historical patterns and stereotypes; understanding this enables proactive prevention.
Diversity & inclusionPushes teams to be a microcosm of diversity → more comprehensive system design.
Social progress & long-term viabilityFair AI bridges disparities and champions equal opportunity; equitable systems enable sustainable adoption in health care, education and finance.

3. How Bias Can Impact Decision-Making Processes

  • Distorted perceptions — bias changes how information is perceived; some aspects over-weighted, others missed → skewed understanding.
  • Unconscious (implicit) biases — cultural, societal and personal factors shape judgments even in well-intentioned people.
  • Confirmation bias — favouring information that confirms existing beliefs; contradictory information is ignored.
  • Stereotyping — assumptions about a person based on group membership instead of individual merit.
  • Unequal treatment — different opportunities, resources or punishments by race, gender, socioeconomic status.
  • Discriminatory outcomes — at individual and systemic levels: access to education, employment, health care.
  • Impact on automated systems — biased training data → biased predictions; entrenches inequality.
  • Feedback loops — biased decisions limit a group's opportunities, reinforcing stereotypes and further marginalising them.
  • Erosion of trust — perceived bias damages trust in institutions, can cause social unrest.
  • Reinforcing inequalities — groups that keep facing biased decisions stay in a cycle of disadvantage.

4. Types of Bias

Bias in ML = systematic, unfair errors in data or models that lead to inaccurate or unjust predictions. It can appear at different stages of the ML pipeline (Fig. 2-1).

CategoryTypeMeaning / example
1. Data biasSampling biasCollected data isn't representative of the population → over/under-representation. E.g. a medical dataset with only one demographic group → poor performance on others.
Measurement biasErrors or inconsistencies in data collection/measurement. E.g. a survey in a language a community doesn't understand omits their view.
Coverage biasCertain groups or perspectives are missing (biased collection methods, incomplete sampling, systemic exclusion).
2. Model bias
(from reliance on biased data in training; may be amplified)		Representation biasTraining features/attributes disproportionately favour certain groups; the model learns those biases.
Algorithmic biasSome algorithms inherently perpetuate bias, e.g. a decision tree splitting on biased features reflects them in predictions.
Feedback-loop biasPredictions influence real-world decisions that shape future training data → bias reinforced over time.
3. Social bias
(society's biases reflected in data and models)		Cultural biasCultural norms, beliefs and values shape how data is collected and interpreted.
Gender biasHistorical gender roles → unequal representation in datasets, affecting performance.
Racial biasBiased historical practices → under- or misrepresentation of racial groups.
Economic biasSocioeconomic disparities → differences in data availability and quality.

5. Examples of Real-World Cases Where Models Exhibited Bias

CaseWhat happened
1. Amazon's gender-biased hiring tool (2018)AI recruiting tool trained on ten years of resumes, mostly from men → learned to favour male applicants and downgrade resumes containing terms associated with women.
2. Racial bias in criminal risk assessmentTools predicting reoffending from historical arrest and conviction data overestimate risk for minority groups, leading to discriminatory sentencing and parole decisions.
3. Google Photos racist labelling (2015)Auto-tagging labelled images of Black people as "gorillas" — training data lacked diverse examples; shows the need for inclusive datasets.
4. Biased loan-approval modelsSome models unfairly denied loans to minority applicants or offered higher interest rates, reflecting historical bias in lending data.
5. Facial recognition & racial biasMore likely to misidentify people with darker skin tones, particularly women → inaccurate surveillance, racial profiling, infringement of civil rights.
TakeawayCurate diverse, representative training data, use fairness-aware algorithms, apply bias detection & mitigation, and continuously monitor model outputs for fairness.

6. Techniques to Detect Bias

TechniqueHow it detects bias
Exploratory data analysis (EDA)Visualise distributions and patterns across groups/attributes to spot disparities.
Fairness metricsQuantify bias in predictions — disparate impact, equal opportunity difference, statistical parity difference — comparing outcomes between groups.
Benchmark datasetsDatasets with intentionally present fairness issues, to test how well models handle bias.
Group disparity analysisCompare outcomes (acceptance rates, loan approvals, hiring) across groups and attributes.
Sensitivity analysisTest how small changes in inputs affect outcomes; reveals features driving biased predictions.
Adversarial testingDeliberately feed biased inputs to see how the model responds and how susceptible it is.
Real-world performance analysisMonitor deployed models continuously to catch emerging bias patterns.
Proxy variable analysisCheck attributes correlated with protected characteristics (gender, race) — they can hide bias.
Interpretability techniquesE.g. feature-importance analysis shows which features drive predictions; disproportionate biased features signal bias.
Human evaluation & feedbackDiverse reviewers inspect outputs and spot bias automated checks miss.
Fairness auditsComprehensive review of data collection, preprocessing and model development.
Synthetic testing scenariosControlled synthetic data simulates potential bias sources to observe their impact.
Key fairness metrics Statistical parity difference = P(Ŷ=1 | unprivileged) − P(Ŷ=1 | privileged)  (ideal 0) • Disparate impact = P(Ŷ=1 | unprivileged) ÷ P(Ŷ=1 | privileged)  (ideal 1; < 0.8 is flagged) • Equal opportunity difference = TPRunprivileged − TPRprivileged  (ideal 0) • Average odds difference = mean of the FPR and TPR differences between groups  (ideal 0).

7. Techniques to Mitigate Bias

#StrategyDetail
1Re-samplingBalance class representation: oversample under-represented or undersample over-represented groups.
2Re-weightingAssign different weights to classes/samples to address imbalance in learning.
3Fairness-aware algorithmsAdversarial debiasing (an adversarial network forces the main model to disregard bias-correlated features); equalised odds (adjust thresholds for equal opportunity across groups); reject-option classification (decline to predict when fairness is uncertain).
4Regularisation techniquesFairness constraints in the optimisation; Lagrangian relaxation to balance fairness vs accuracy via Lagrange multipliers.
5Post-processing interventionsCalibration of predictions to fairness criteria; re-ranking predictions to promote fairness without hurting accuracy.
6Pre-processing interventionsData augmentation (synthesised data for under-represented groups); de-biasing preprocessing (re-weighting / re-sampling before training).
7Fair feature engineeringCreate/select features less correlated with bias.
8Ensemble methodsCombine models trained with different strategies — biases in individual models are less likely to coincide.
9Regular monitoring & updatesKeep checking real-world performance and update the model as new data arrives.
10Ethical & inclusive designDiverse representation and ethical considerations in data collection, preprocessing and model development.
11Collaborative developmentInvolve stakeholders from diverse backgrounds, ethicists and affected communities.
12Transparency & communicationBe open with users and stakeholders about mitigation steps to build trust.
13Legal & regulatory complianceAdhere to anti-discrimination and bias laws.
Pre-processing (data) → In-processing (algorithm / constraints) → Post-processing (outputs)

8. Implementing Bias Detection & Fairness — Stage 1: Data Bias

Goal: train on the original data, detect racial bias by analysing it, then resample for a fair representation and retrain. Dataset: UCI Adult census income data (48,842 rows, 14 features + target income >50k / ≤50k) — age, workclass, education, marital-status, occupation, relationship, race, sex, capital-gain/loss, hours-per-week, native-country.

Import → Load CSV → Check (info) → Encode → Plot distributions → RandomForest → Resample → Retrain
  1. Packages: pandas, numpy, train_test_split, RandomForestClassifier, accuracy/classification_report, sklearn.utils.resample, LabelEncoder.
  2. Load & check: df = pd.read_csv("Income.csv"), df.info() → no null values.
  3. Preprocess: label-encode categorical columns; one-hot (pd.get_dummies) columns with more than two categories.
  4. Explore: value-count bar plots show the imbalance (below).
x = df.drop(['income'], axis=1);  y = df['income']
x_train, x_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(x, y, test_size=0.2, random_state=42)
model = RandomForestClassifier(random_state=42).fit(x_train, y_train)
print(classification_report(model.predict(x_test), y_test))   # accuracy 0.8526

# Mitigate: upsample the minority race group (race == 0, Black) to match the majority
black = df[df['race'] == 0];  white = df[df['race'] == 1]
upscaled_black = resample(black, replace=True, n_samples=len(white), random_state=42)
balanced_data = pd.concat([upscaled_black, white])
# ...split balanced_data and retrain the same RandomForest
ModelAccuracyF1 (class 0 / class 1)Observation
Original (biased) data85.3%0.91 / 0.67Much weaker on the minority/positive class due to imbalance
After resampling97.4%0.98 / 0.93Class-1 precision 0.92 and recall 0.95 — large improvement
Exam-smart caution (not in the book)The 97% is inflated: upsampling with replacement before the train/test split puts duplicate rows in both sets (data leakage). In practice, split first and resample only the training set.

9. Implementing — Stage 2: Model Bias (AI Fairness 360)

Goal: detect and mitigate bias in the model using IBM AIF360's Adversarial Debiasing — trains two neural networks adversarially: a classifier predicts the label while an adversary tries to predict the protected attribute from the output; the classifier is penalised if it can, which removes dependence on the protected attribute. Works for binary classification with a binary protected attribute (gender, race, age). It is promising but only one solution.

Import AIF360 → Load preprocessed Adult → Train plain model (debias=False) → Train debiased model (debias=True) → Compare metrics
from aif360.datasets import AdultDataset
from aif360.metrics import ClassificationMetric
from aif360.algorithms.inprocessing.adversarial_debiasing import AdversarialDebiasing
import tensorflow.compat.v1 as tf;  tf.disable_eager_execution()

df = load_preproc_data_adult()
privileged   = [{'sex': 1}];  unprivileged = [{'sex': 0}]
df_train, df_test = df.split([0.7], shuffle=True)

session = tf.Session()
plain = AdversarialDebiasing(privileged_groups=privileged, unprivileged_groups=unprivileged,
                             scope_name='plain_classifier', debias=False, sess=session)
plain.fit(df_train)                       # ... then close session, reset graph

session = tf.Session()
debiased = AdversarialDebiasing(privileged_groups=privileged, unprivileged_groups=unprivileged,
                                scope_name='debiased_classifier', debias=True, sess=session)
debiased.fit(df_train)
m = ClassificationMetric(df_test, debiased.predict(df_test),
        unprivileged_groups=unprivileged, privileged_groups=privileged)
m.accuracy(); m.disparate_impact(); m.equal_opportunity_difference(); m.average_odds_difference()

InterpretationDebiasing sharply reduces the gap in true-positive rates and in true/false-positive rates between protected and unprotected groups, at only a small accuracy cost (0.809 → 0.796) — the fairness–accuracy trade-off. The book notes the metrics improve though accuracy slightly drops.

10. Quick Revision & Likely Questions

One-liners
  • Bias = systematic deviation causing unfair outcomes; from data collection, model construction, history, society.
  • Types: Data (sampling, measurement, coverage) • Model (representation, algorithmic, feedback loop) • Social (cultural, gender, racial, economic).
  • Impact: distorted perception, stereotyping, unequal treatment, feedback loops, erosion of trust, reinforced inequality.
  • Cases: Amazon hiring • criminal risk assessment • Google Photos • loan approval • facial recognition.
  • Detect: EDA, fairness metrics (disparate impact, equal opportunity diff., statistical parity diff.), group disparity, sensitivity, adversarial testing, proxy analysis, interpretability, audits.
  • Mitigate: re-sampling / re-weighting (pre), adversarial debiasing / equalised odds / constraints (in), calibration / re-ranking / reject option (post) + diverse teams, monitoring, compliance.
  • Implementation: Stage 1 resampling on Adult data (F1 for class 1: 0.67 → 0.93); Stage 2 AIF360 adversarial debiasing (disparate impact 0 → 0.56).
